Warning Signs

When should you upgrade your switchboard?

If your switchboard is old, damaged, overheating or missing modern protection, it should be inspected before more load is added.

Old ceramic fuses are still installed

No safety switches protecting circuits

Safety switch or breaker keeps tripping

Burning smell near the switchboard

Buzzing, crackling or heat at the board

Lights flicker when appliances turn on

Breakers trip when ovens, hot water, EV chargers or air conditioning start

Switchboard looks overcrowded or damaged

You are renovating or adding new circuits

A switchboard upgrade should make the electrical system safer, easier to understand and better prepared for modern loads.

Modern safety switch and RCBO protection where suitable

Clearer circuit labelling for easier fault finding

Reduced risk from old ceramic fuses and damaged fittings

Better preparation for renovations, EV chargers and added circuits

Switchboard authority

The board should be ready for the way the property is used now.

A good upgrade is not just a tidy row of breakers. It should consider old protection, heat damage, overloaded circuits, future loads and whether the supply needs a deeper Level 2 review.

Old fuses and missing protection

Ceramic fuse boards can be harder to isolate and may not provide the modern safety switch or RCBO protection expected on upgraded circuits.

Burnt wiring and heat damage

Heat marks, buzzing, melted insulation or a burning smell need proper inspection before the affected circuit is placed back into normal use.

Overloaded circuits

Repeated tripping can come from heavy appliances, added circuits, poor load balance, old wiring or equipment that needs a dedicated supply.

EV, three-phase and new loads

EV chargers, workshops, renovations and larger appliances should be checked against the existing switchboard, protection and supply capacity.

Switchboard FAQ

Common switchboard questions.

Do ceramic fuses need to be replaced?

Older ceramic fuse boards should be inspected because they may not provide the same level of modern circuit protection expected in upgraded switchboards.

Why does my switchboard keep tripping?

Repeated tripping can come from a circuit fault, appliance issue, water ingress, overloaded circuit or damaged wiring. The cause should be tested before parts are replaced.

Can a switchboard upgrade help with EV chargers or renovations?

Often yes. New loads may require circuit, protection or supply capacity checks before the property is ready for the extra demand.
